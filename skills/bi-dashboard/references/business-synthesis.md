# Business Synthesis

Use this reference when writing insight panel items, action panel items, headline assessments, and KPI notes. Mandatory rules stay in `../SKILL.md`; this file gives writing patterns.

## Insight Structure

Every insight and action must follow this three-part pattern:

**Finding → Meaning → Action**

- **Finding**: what the data specifically shows — a number, rate, count, trend, or comparison.
- **Meaning**: why that finding matters to the business — the implication or risk.
- **Action**: what to do about it — names a segment, owner focus, or measurable target.

Example:

```
Finding:  Partner referrals produce 18% of leads but 34% of won opportunities.
Meaning:  This source yields nearly twice the conversion quality of other channels.
Action:   Expand partner referral budget and fast-track these leads through qualification.
```

Insight panel items state the finding and meaning.
Action panel items state the action, tied back to the finding.

## Confidence Language

Match wording to the strength of the data:

| Data quality | Wording |
|---|---|
| Large sample, clear signal | "The data clearly shows…" |
| Meaningful pattern, smaller sample | "The analysis suggests…" |
| Sparse data, early signal | "There are early indications that…" |

Do not claim strong findings from thin or sparse categories.

## "So What?" Rule

Every finding must connect to a business implication. Do not stop at reporting a number.

Weak:
```
Paid social contributes 31% of leads.
```

Better:
```
Paid social contributes 31% of leads but only 9% of won opportunities —
suggesting a qualification problem that is diluting pipeline quality.
```

## Business Language

Use concrete numbers, percentages, and named segments. Avoid vague language.

| Instead of | Use |
|---|---|
| Performance varies | [Source A] converts at 2x the rate of [Source B] |
| Significant increase | Revenue grew 23% month-over-month |
| Monitor closely | Review [segment] qualification rules this week |
| Low-performing area | [Stage/source/segment] has [X]% higher drop-off than peers |

## Headline Assessment

The headline card should answer in one or two sentences:
- What is the current state?
- Is it healthy, at risk, improving, or deteriorating?

Use a specific number. Avoid generic openings like "This dashboard shows…"

Weak:
```
This dashboard provides an overview of sales pipeline performance.
```

Better:
```
Pipeline health is under pressure: 38% of open opportunities are stale,
concentrated in the Proposal and Negotiation stages.
```
