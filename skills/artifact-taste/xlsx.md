# Taste for workbooks

A workbook gets the smallest creativity budget: it must be usable, readable and correct first. Its originality is in how the information is organised and how a reader moves through it, not in colour. A quiet workbook can be the best one: most sheets need the grid, one header, and a conditional format or a small touch only where a value needs to be seen.

## A workbook can be

A model (inputs, calculations, outputs), a decision surface, a work queue, a ledger, a scenario lab, a notebook of analysis.

## Usability checklist

- Inputs, calculations and outputs are kept apart, and the reader can tell which is which (the kit's blue inputs and its one-line legend). Results stay in plain ink: a colour on a number should mean something the reader needs.
- One header style per sheet; headers frozen; column widths fit their content; units in the headers.
- One number format per kind of value; percentages as percentages, money as money.
- No merged cells inside data; no blank spacer columns inside a table.
- Colour only means something (inputs, totals, warnings), never decoration.
- Charts answer one question each and sit next to their data; print setup is done.
- Five or more sheets: an overview sheet that links to each.

## Plain sheet or dashboard

Most workbooks need no dashboard. Make a dashboard page when the brief asks for one, or when one reader keeps coming back to the same data with a different question each time and reads rather than edits. A reader who picks nothing can still have one, as a static page, if a one-page view is what was asked for. Do not make one for a list someone works through, a model someone edits, or a summary with one answer: those are better as a filtered table, inputs and outputs, or a short sheet. The data sheets behind a dashboard stay plain.

## Dials that apply here

Readability outranks every dial: keep the filled header band, frozen headers, the input colour and legible sizes.
