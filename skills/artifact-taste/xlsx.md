# Taste for workbooks

A workbook gets the smallest creativity budget: it must be usable, readable and correct first. Its originality is in how the information is organised and how a reader moves through it, not in colour. A quiet workbook can be the best one.

## A workbook can be

A model (inputs, calculations, outputs), a decision surface, a work queue, a ledger, a scenario lab, a notebook of analysis.

## Usability checklist

- Inputs, calculations and outputs are kept apart, and the reader can tell which is which (the kit's blue inputs and legend).
- One header style per sheet; headers frozen; column widths fit their content; units in the headers.
- One number format per kind of value; percentages as percentages, money as money.
- No merged cells inside data; no blank spacer columns inside a table.
- Colour only means something (inputs, totals, warnings), never decoration.
- Charts answer one question each and sit next to their data; print setup is done.
- Five or more sheets: an overview sheet that links to each.

## Your workbook attractors

A dashboard sheet nobody asked for; every metric turned into a chart; KPI tiles above a table that already shows the numbers.

## Dials that apply here

Only density (row height, column spacing), the header treatment (accent fill or a rule under plain text) and gridlines on or off. Everything else stays plain.
