# Taste for Word documents

Word gets a modest creativity budget, on purpose: a Word file is meant to be edited, printed and read in order, and its layout options are limited. The craft is structure and typography. A well-made document can look quiet and still be excellent.

## A Word document can be

A memo, a field report, a proposal, a handbook, a dossier, a policy; it can lead with figures, carry annotated tables as its main thread, or use short sections with a running summary.

## Structure is the design

- Real heading styles, at most three levels, so navigation and a contents page work.
- Spacing comes from styles, never from blank lines or repeated breaks.
- Tables carry data, never page layout; every table and figure has a caption.
- Lists for steps and sets, prose for argument; no random bold, no fake headings made of bold text.
- Page breaks only before real sections.

## Your Word attractors

A coloured title with a rule under it; a tinted callout box in every section; a row of figure tiles at the top; every heading in the accent colour. Use them only when the content asks.

## Dials that apply here

- Type voice: the heading and body faces (Arial or Times New Roman) and the contrast between them.
- Contrast: heading colour against body colour; how dark the table header is.
- Line: no rules, a rule under the title, rules between sections.
- Density: body size, line spacing and paragraph spacing.

Shape, ground, alignment and colour fields mostly do not apply to Word.

## Craft floor

Body text 10.5pt or more; one font pairing; numbering never typed by hand; "Page X of Y" stays correct.
